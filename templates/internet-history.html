Introduction

The Internet is a global network that connects computers and devices so they can communicate and exchange information. It started from early research networks such as ARPANET and developed over many years. Technologies such as TCP/IP and DNS later helped the Internet expand and connect devices around the world.

Key Milestones

1969 - ARPANET Begins

The first ARPANET connections were established in 1969. By the end of the year, four computers were connected to the network.

Why it mattered: ARPANET proved that computers in different places could communicate with each other through a network.

Source: Internet Society

1972 - Email Is Introduced on ARPANET

Electronic mail became an important application on ARPANET. Ray Tomlinson developed basic software for sending and reading email messages.

Why it mattered: Email made it possible for people to send messages to each other using a computer network.

Source: Internet Society

1973 - Development of Internet Networking Protocols Begins

Research began on connecting different types of computer networks. Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn worked on ideas that later developed into TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: This work allowed different computer networks to connect and exchange information.

Source: Internet Society

1983 - ARPANET Changes to TCP/IP

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from its previous protocol to TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: TCP/IP became the main set of rules used for communication between networks and is still used on the Internet today.

Source: Internet Society

1983 - Domain Name System Is Developed

DNS was created to make it easier to find computers and services on the Internet.

Why it mattered: It lets people use simple domain names instead of having to remember numerical IP addresses.

Source: Internet Society

1986 - NSFNET Is Launched

The U.S. National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect researchers and supercomputer centers.

Why it mattered: NSFNET helped connect more universities and research centers and contributed to the growth of the Internet.

Source: National Science Foundation

1990 - ARPANET Is Decommissioned

ARPANET was shut down after about two decades of operation.

Why it mattered: By this time, the Internet had already grown beyond ARPANET, so the original network was no longer needed.

Source: Internet Society

1995 - NSFNET Backbone Is Retired

The NSFNET backbone was shut down as commercial Internet networks continued to expand.

Why it mattered: This showed the move from a mainly research-based Internet toward an Internet operated more by commercial companies.

Source: National Science Foundation

Three Key Technologies

Packet Switching

Packet switching breaks data into small packets before sending it through a network. The packets can travel separately and are put back together when they reach the destination.

TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a set of rules that allows devices to communicate over the Internet. IP handles addressing and routing, while TCP helps make sure the information arrives correctly.

DNS - Domain Name System

DNS connects domain names to IP addresses. This means people can type an easy website name instead of remembering a numerical address.
