History of the Internet

Introduction

The Internet is a global system of connected computer networks. It allows computers, phones, servers, and other devices around the world to communicate and exchange information. It developed gradually from research networks such as ARPANET into the worldwide infrastructure used today. The Internet is not the same as the World Wide Web. The Internet is the network infrastructure, while the Web is one of the services that works on top of it.

Key Milestones

1969 – ARPANET Begins

The first ARPANET connections were established in 1969. By the end of the year, four computers were connected to the network.

Why it mattered: ARPANET showed that computers in different locations could communicate through a network.

Source: Internet Society – A Brief History of the Internet

1972 – Email Becomes an Important Network Application

Email became one of the first widely useful applications on ARPANET. Ray Tomlinson developed software for sending and reading electronic messages.

Why it mattered: Email showed that computer networks could be used for direct communication between people.

Source: Internet Society – A Brief History of the Internet

1973 – Research on Internetworking Begins

Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn worked on methods for connecting different computer networks. Their work contributed to the development of TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: Different networks could communicate with each other instead of remaining isolated.

Source: Internet Society – A Brief History of the Internet

1983 – ARPANET Changes to TCP/IP

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from its previous protocol to TCP/IP.

Why it mattered: TCP/IP provided a common communication system that allowed different networks and computers to work together. It remains fundamental to the Internet today.

Source: Internet Society – A Brief History of the Internet

1983 – The Domain Name System Is Developed

The Domain Name System, or DNS, was developed to connect human-readable domain names with numerical Internet addresses.

Why it mattered: DNS made Internet resources easier to access because users no longer needed to remember numerical IP addresses.

Source: ICANN – Domain Name System history and resources

1986 – NSFNET Is Launched

The U.S. National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect academic researchers and supercomputer centers.

Why it mattered: NSFNET expanded network access to more universities and researchers and became an important part of the growing Internet infrastructure.

Source: U.S. National Science Foundation – Birth of the Commercial Internet

1990 – ARPANET Is Decommissioned

ARPANET was shut down in 1990 after about two decades of operation.

Why it mattered: By that time, the Internet had grown far beyond the original ARPANET, and TCP/IP had become widely used.

Source: Internet Society – A Brief History of the Internet

1995 – NSFNET Backbone Is Retired

The NSFNET backbone was shut down in 1995 as commercial Internet providers expanded their own networks.

Why it mattered: This marked an important transition from a mainly research-based Internet toward a commercial and public Internet.

Source: U.S. National Science Foundation – Birth of the Commercial Internet

1998 – ICANN Is Established

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers, or ICANN, was founded in 1998 to help coordinate the Domain Name System and other unique Internet identifiers.

Why it mattered: As the Internet became global, it needed an organized system for coordinating important resources such as domain names and Internet identifiers.

Source: ICANN – History Project

2000s – Broadband and Mobile Internet Grow Rapidly

During the 2000s, broadband, Wi-Fi, mobile networks, and smartphones greatly increased Internet access.

Why it mattered: The Internet became faster, more widely available, and increasingly accessible through mobile devices instead of only desktop computers.

Source: International Telecommunication Union – historical telecommunications and Internet development data

2012 – World IPv6 Launch

On June 6, 2012, major websites, Internet service providers, and equipment manufacturers permanently enabled IPv6.

Why it mattered: IPv6 provides a much larger number of Internet addresses than IPv4, helping the Internet continue to grow as more people and devices connect.

Source: Internet Society – World IPv6 Launch

2016 – IANA Stewardship Transition

On October 1, 2016, the U.S. government’s IANA functions contract ended, completing the transition of stewardship of important Internet identifiers to the global multistakeholder community.

Why it mattered: It changed how key global Internet resources are coordinated and reduced direct U.S. government oversight of the IANA functions.

Source: ICANN – IANA Stewardship Transition

2025–2026 – The Internet Today

The Internet now connects billions of people through broadband, Wi-Fi, fibre, mobile networks, and other technologies. In 2025, the International Telecommunication Union estimated that about 6 billion people were using the Internet.

Why it matters: The Internet has developed from a small research network into essential global infrastructure used for communication, education, healthcare, business, entertainment, cloud services, and many other activities.

Source: International Telecommunication Union – Facts and Figures 2025

Three Key Internet Technologies

  1. Packet Switching

    Packet switching divides information into smaller packets that can travel through a network separately and are reassembled when they reach their destination.

  2. TCP/IP

    TCP/IP is the main collection of communication protocols used by the Internet. IP handles addressing and routing, while TCP helps provide reliable delivery of information between devices.

  3. DNS – Domain Name System

    DNS connects human-readable domain names with numerical IP addresses, making Internet resources easier to locate and use.
