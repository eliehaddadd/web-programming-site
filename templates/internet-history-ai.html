Overview

The Internet developed from early research networks that allowed computers in different locations to communicate. ARPANET was one of the most important early networks. Later, technologies such as TCP/IP and DNS helped connect more networks and devices around the world.

Internet Timeline

  1. 1969 - ARPANET Begins

    The first ARPANET connections were created in 1969, and four computers were connected by the end of the year.

    Why it mattered: It showed that computers located in different places could communicate through a computer network.

    Reference: Internet Society

  2. 1972 - Email Is Introduced on ARPANET

    Email became an important ARPANET application. Ray Tomlinson developed software that allowed users to send and read electronic messages.

    Why it mattered: Email gave people a useful way to communicate with each other through a computer network.

    Reference: Internet Society

  3. 1973 - Internet Networking Protocol Development Begins

    Research focused on connecting different types of computer networks. Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn worked on ideas that later became TCP/IP.

    Why it mattered: Their work helped different networks connect and exchange information with one another.

    Reference: Internet Society

  4. 1983 - ARPANET Changes to TCP/IP

    On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from its previous protocol to TCP/IP.

    Why it mattered: TCP/IP became the main communication system used to connect different networks and remains important to the Internet today.

    Reference: Internet Society

  5. 1983 - Domain Name System Is Developed

    DNS was developed to make computers and services on the Internet easier to locate.

    Why it mattered: It allowed users to work with simple domain names instead of remembering numerical IP addresses.

    Reference: Internet Society

  6. 1986 - NSFNET Is Launched

    The U.S. National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect researchers and supercomputer centers.

    Why it mattered: NSFNET connected more universities and research centers and supported the continued growth of the Internet.

    Reference: National Science Foundation

  7. 1990 - ARPANET Is Decommissioned

    ARPANET was shut down after operating for about two decades.

    Why it mattered: The Internet had already developed beyond ARPANET, so the original network was no longer necessary.

    Reference: Internet Society

  8. 1995 - NSFNET Backbone Is Retired

    The NSFNET backbone was retired while commercial Internet networks continued to grow.

    Why it mattered: This represented the move from a mainly research-based Internet toward one operated increasingly by commercial companies.

    Reference: National Science Foundation

Important Internet Technologies

Packet Switching
Packet switching divides data into smaller packets before sending it across a network. The packets are combined again after reaching their destination.
TCP/IP
TCP/IP is a set of communication rules used by devices on the Internet. IP manages addressing and routing, while TCP helps ensure that data arrives correctly.
DNS - Domain Name System
DNS connects domain names with IP addresses. It allows people to use easy website names instead of remembering numerical addresses.
